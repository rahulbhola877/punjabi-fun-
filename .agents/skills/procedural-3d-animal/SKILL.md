# Procedural 3D animal — detailed (Low-Poly Menagerie series)

Use this skill for **detailed** animals. For **low-poly / lightweight / game-ready** animals use the **low-poly-animal** skill instead (one smooth skin, painted colours, relevant moves only).

Goal: one self-contained HTML page per animal. Everything is generated in code: no Meshy, no model files, no 3D libraries. Output: an interactive page (publish it as an artifact if the session can; otherwise send the .html file) that the user can orbit, with action buttons and face buttons.

## Workflow
1. Copy the closest template to a new folder as the base. Only the animal-specific sections change (listed below); the engine stays the same.
2. Edit with a Python make.py that replaces text between markers (`// ===== X SCULPT =====` → `function grad(`, `// ---------- rig ----------` → `const COL={`, `const COL={` → the `/* === Scene */` block comment, `function buildFace(){` → `function eH(p)`, `const LL=` → `const EXPR={`). Keep the Scene block between buildPig and buildFace; deleting it once caused `pigItem is not defined`. Put every later tweak into make.py too, so a re-run doesn't lose it.
3. Render-test in headless Chromium (see Testing), fix the problems, then publish (a new artifact per animal; slug + base_version for revisions). Reply briefly.

## Engine (in every template, hand-written WebGL2)
- Math: column-major mat4 helpers (euler XYZ matching three.js, trs, basis/lookAt, persp, ortho, inv). Scene-graph `Node` with p, r, s and an optional rm (base rotation matrix).
- Geometry generators: sphere (three.js convention), cyl, icosa, box, disc, torus, catmull-rom tube with taper, flatten (per-face colours; defaults to white when the geometry has no colours, otherwise particles render as dark grey blobs), jitter.
- Main shader: GPU skinning (uBones[24] in pangolin and leopard, [20] in pig and bear; raise the array and `boneMats` together if you add bones), 4 weights, morph targets (aM1/aM2, uMW), smooth or flat normals, 5x5 PCF shadow map, hemisphere light + wrapped sun, specular, rim, fog, ACES tonemapping. Face culling is disabled.
- Attribute locations: 0 pos, 1 normal, 2 colour, 3 skin index, 4 skin weight, 5 rest position / morph 1, 6 morph 2, 7 hair density (on scales: lift amount). `upload()` returns `bufs` so dynamic meshes (snowfall) can use bufferSubData.
- Skin and coat pattern block (runs for both the skin and every fur shell, in rest space vR so it sticks to the body): fbm mottling, plus whatever pattern the species has: freckles (pig), rosettes (leopard: worley rings broken by noise, solid spots on head and legs, blotches on the tail, masked off the belly and muzzle). Stripes would go here too.
- Fur: shell rendering (SHELLS layers along the normal, HAIR_LEN, gravity/drag comb uComb, strand mask from rotated rest-space value noise, lighter tips). Never use floor() grid cells for strands: they cause moiré. Shells are skipped in the shadow pass; SHELLS=0 means no fur.
- Scales (pangolin): `buildScales()` walks a Catmull-Rom centreline by arc length, marches rings out to the surface (dorsal arc; a full ring on the tail; a narrow arc on the head to keep the eyes clear), and offsets alternate rows by half a scale. `addScale()` makes a cupped, keeled, pointed plate tilted 0.2 rad, skinned rigidly with pigWeights(anchor). Flare: the vertex shader adds `n*aHair*uFlare`, and `action.flare(t)` sets it.
- Habitats: meadow `buildEnv()` (pig, bear, pangolin + termite mounds), underwater pond `buildEnv()` (axolotl: teal gradient sky shader, fog 1.5-19, caustics uCaus, water surface + lily pads above, sway shader for plants, bubbles particle kind) or mountains `buildEnv()` (snow leopard: ridged-noise terrain that stays flat for r<8, colour by slope and height (rock / snow / alpine grass), distant snow-capped peaks, snowy pines, snow-topped boulders, edelweiss, drifting snowfall, colder sky and fog). Pick the habitat that matches the species: mountains, savanna, forest, wetland, desert, etc. Keep props and rocks at least 7 units from the centre so the follow camera isn't blocked.
- Particles (dust, hearts, Z letters, droplets), camera shake, orbit controls, follow camera.

## Per-animal sections to rewrite
1. **Sculpt (SDF):** smin unions of ellipsoids, round cones and rounded cylinders: torso, rump, chest, neck, head, cheeks, muzzle, ears (rotated frame via EAR_M, cupped), legs (sdLegF/sdLegB with paws or hooves), tail (a TAILP/TAILR chain of cones for long tails). Mirror with abs(x). Flat bottom: max(d, -(y-0.004)). Faces +z, stands on y=0.
2. **Mesh:** surfaceNets(sdf, bbox, step 0.025-0.034), relax for 3 iterations, orient faces, smooth gradient normals. The result is ONE continuous skin, with no seams or cuts.
3. **Rig:** BN, REST, PARENT and SEG. Weights: inverse distance, top 4. Spine bones get an offset and power 6 for soft bending. Ears, legs and tail are gated to vertices near their SDF part, with a height penalty on the legs. Long tails get 5 bones (t0-t4).
4. **Colours:** pigVertex(p) returns [colour, hairDensity], using smoothstep blends only. No hard borders.
5. **Face** (rigid parts on the head bone): eyes (white, iris, pupil, ring, two highlights, skin lids, lash torus, brow; `bg.r.z = E.ba*side`, where positive means angry), mouth tubes with smile/frown morphs, an open-mouth ellipsoid (FACE.openS), and fangs for predators. Optional extras: tongue node (FACE.tongue), nose (FACE.nos with .base), blush, whiskers, claws on the lower leg bones (retractable claws can be toggled visible from action.fx).
6. **Moves:** POSES loops (idle, walk, run, eat, bend, happy, sleep) plus ACTIONS (dur, pose, speed, face, optional flare and fx(at, fw)). Conventions: +x on a leg = foot back; +x on the head = nose down; hips -x = rear up (compensate the hind legs with +x); hips.z ≈ 1.45 lies the animal on its side. Use crouch with leg length LL. Ball/roll: curl the spine and tail, then add a roll on the root x channel (re-pivoted around BALL_C, `%TAU`). Bipedal (pangolin run): keep it low, with hips about -0.34, bent hind knees and the front paws tucked; a high upright stance looks wrong. Sit (cat happy): hips -0.32, RY -0.27, hind legs folded at -1.25/+2.1, front legs straight. Match the moves to the species: pig headbutt charge; bear rear-up swipe roar slam; pangolin tail lash, curl and roll, low bipedal run; snow leopard stalk-wiggle-pounce, roar, sit-and-purr, stretch.
7. **Expressions:** EXPR presets (neutral, happy, excited, content, angry, roar, surprised, sad, sleepy, asleep, squint). LOOP_FACE and action.face pick them; manual chips override. Add blinks, saccades, and eyes that track the camera.
8. **Tuning reference:** pig SHELLS 12 / HAIR_LEN 0.024; bear 18 / 0.055; snow leopard 16 / 0.042; pangolin 0 with scales; horse 8 / 0.014; eagle 7 / 0.0095 (soft body plumage under the feather cards).
9. **Text and camera:** title, badge, loading text, cam.d and target height for the animal's size (small: d≈3.5, target≈0.42; cat: 3.7, 0.56; bear: 5.2, 0.82). Walk/run speeds and phase rates also scale with size.

## Lessons from animals 07-14
- **Young versions (babyfy.py):** warp FORWARD only: build the adult mesh, then move each vertex with warpF (head bigger, legs shorter, body rounder) and recompute normals. Weights and colours come from the adult positions; build the face in adult space under a scaled face node. Never invert the warp inside the SDF (Newton inverse): it makes sheets and folds.
- **Ground contact (ground.py `add_ground`):** after posing, find the lowest paw/hoof contact point and shift the root so it touches y=0 (skip for jumps/prances), plus soft contact-shadow blobs under each foot. Expose `window.__skin` and `bm()` so tests can measure the skinned minimum y.
- **Real proportions first:** search for reference photos before sculpting (a house cat is not a small leopard; a horse head has a broad flat forehead, round cheeks/jowls, a deep jaw, a square muzzle and a clearly narrower throatlatch: a smooth tapering tube reads as an anteater).
- **Coats via uniforms:** vertex colour carries masks only ([shade, pointsMask, white/bellyMask]); the colours come from uniforms (uCoatA, uCoatB, ...), so a Coat/Colour row recolours instantly without rebuilding the mesh.
- **Breeds/varieties:** a breed is a small parameter object: overall size and width (root node scale), neck length (scale the head bone's local offset), leg length (scale the lower-leg bone offsets), head profile (a 2nd morph target made by pushing vertices along the normal with a gaussian on the nose bridge: +1 dished Arabian, negative = Roman nose; remember to pass pigItem.mw to the fur shells too), mane/tail variant items toggled visible, extra hair items (feathering), tail carriage, head carriage, knee action, coat gloss and its default coat. Match the user's reference: an Akhal-Teke is pale champagne-gold with a metallic sheen (specular tinted by the coat + broad warm lobe + rim, no fur shells), not yellow.
- **Tails at speed:** a hanging tail must swing back and up (+x on the tail root) when galloping, trotting or prancing; never tuck it under.
- **Birds (eagle template):** rest pose = flight T-pose (body level, wings spread flat, legs hanging); perch by pitching the root up and counter-rotating the legs. Wing = shoulder (orients the wing plane), humerus, forearm, hand; folding is in-plane rotation at elbow and wrist. Flight feathers are separate cards rigid on their bone, each with a 3-state morph (spread / half / folded, aPos/aM1/aM2) that swings it about its own root; drive the morph from the elbow/wrist angle. Add coverts, underwing coverts, scapulars and flank feathers to hide the arm. Altitude is state (eased, rate-limited), ground loops descend through a landing flare pose, bank into turns, and the camera distance grows with altitude. Raptor heads hold still then snap to a new look direction.
- **Glow and fire (phoenix):** an emissive term added after lighting (feather glow rising toward the tips via the per-vertex length coordinate in aHair), flicker on a global uniform, flame particles that age from tip colour to flame colour, embers, flash on the sun colour. A dramatic special (Rise from ashes) is a timeline of functions: scale(t), fire(t), ash(t), fireball(t), flash(t) and spawn rules, plus a seq() of poses with cross-fades.
- **Distinct species, shared rig:** when reusing a template for a different creature, change the anatomy too (proportions, neck, head size, beak, legs), not only colours; the user notices a recoloured eagle.
- When adding pose offsets on top of a base pose (perch() already folds the wings), add deltas, not absolute values (f:.88 on top of f:1 gives 1.88 and flips the wing).

## Toon asset mode (default from animal 15 on)
The deliverable is a **toon asset**, not a scene. Background/habitat is optional: default to a neutral studio backdrop with a soft floor and a **Background** toggle that shows the habitat. Every page gets an **Asset** row that downloads the animal as:
- **GLB** and **glTF** (2.0): skinned mesh + skeleton (bone nodes) + rigid face parts as child nodes of the head joint + morph targets (mouth) + every move baked as an animation clip (in place, 30 fps; loops get an end-to-start blend so they cycle seamlessly). Vertex colours (COLOR_0, linear), PBR materials, KHR_materials_emissive_strength for highlights, KHR_materials_unlit for outlines. Hidden-then-shown parts (tongue, fangs) animate scale 0↔1.
- **FBX 7.4 ASCII**: LimbNode skeleton, Skin + Clusters (TransformLink = bone bind matrix), BindPose, BlendShape channels, one AnimationStack per clip (Lcl T/R/S curves, rotations as FBX XYZ = three 'ZYX' Euler, unwrapped), vertex colours written in sRGB. FBX ASCII is indentation-sensitive for three's loader: nested arrays need the exact tab depth and long arrays continue on lines with NO leading whitespace.
- **three.js r187 starter** (.html): import map to three@0.187.0, GLTFLoader + OrbitControls + AnimationMixer, clip buttons, MeshToonMaterial with a 3-step gradient map, GLB embedded as base64.
- Use `templates/asset-kit.js`: inject it before `function init(){`, define `ASSET_NAME`, keep CPU copies in `upload()` (`return {...,src:{g,extra}}`), call `kitStudio()` after `buildEnv()` (and put the env items in `KIT.env`), `kitBuildUI()` in init. Clip names come from the ACTS labels.
- **Artifact downloads:** the viewer only saves allowlisted extensions (zip, html, json, png…), never .glb/.fbx directly. Declare the `downloads` capability (declare_capabilities `{downloads:true}`), save through `await claude.use('downloads')` → `save({filename,data})` at click time, and wrap .glb/.gltf/.fbx in a .zip (store-only zip writer is in the kit; fallback to a blob link outside the viewer).
- **Everything visible must be geometry** so the file matches the viewer: fur shells are a shader trick and don't export, so soft fur is made of skinned triangle blades grown from the skin (area × hair-density sampling, lie along a comb direction, base darker / tip lighter, winding matched to the surface normal). Long feathering = clumps (tapered wavy tubes). Patterns are baked into vertex colours (keep the masks on the CPU so the Coat row can recolour).
- **Verify exports with three.js r187's own loaders**: the npm registry is blocked, but `git clone --depth 1 --branch r187 --filter=blob:none --sparse https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js` + sparse-checkout `/build/ /examples/jsm/` works. Serve with `python3 -m http.server`, load GLB/glTF/FBX in Playwright, check meshes/skinned/bones/clips and render a posed frame. Also make the export winding follow the normals (engines flip back-face normals when double-sided, which turns inverted parts dark).
- Report the real triangle count. The detailed animals are mid-poly (40–70k tris with fur); call them low-poly only in the low-poly version.

## Low poly
For any low-poly, lightweight or game-ready version (toon low poly or stylised realistic, one smooth skin, painted colours, only relevant moves) switch to the **low-poly-animal** skill. Its reference pages are the 15b retriever and the 01b spotted pig in the table above.

## GLSL gotcha
`patch`, `sample`, `input`, `output`, `filter`, `active`, `common`, `partition` and other reserved words can't be variable names in WebGL2 shaders: the page silently fails to build. Name them e.g. `mpatch`.

## Testing
- Use Playwright + Chromium with args `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`. Abort font requests. Wait for `window.__tris`. If Three.js or other CDNs can't be reached from the sandbox, that doesn't matter: the engine is library-free.
- SwiftShader is very slow (especially with fur). `window.__pig.sim(seconds)` steps the simulation deterministically. `window.__noFur=1` skips the shells. `window.__camT=[x,y,z]` pins the camera target for close-ups. A `rel` camera spec in test-sim.js gives side and front views relative to the animal's heading. Keep viewports around 420-640px and screenshot timeouts at 180 s.
- Check positions numerically (for example mouth or tongue tip y ≥ 0).
- Expressions blend slowly at low frame rates: set `st.manual` and `Object.assign(st.E, EXPR[name])` before capturing.
- Check: idle front and side views, walk, run, attack mid-action, specials, eat, jump, sleep, face close-ups, and a wide shot of the habitat.

## Style the user wants
Toon asset first (studio background, habitat optional, downloadable GLB/glTF/FBX/three.js r187 with all moves), HD quality, one smooth continuous skin (no visible cuts), natural hair, scales, markings and dots, expressive eyes and face, many moves that suit the species, and a habitat that fits the animal (no snow leopard in a meadow). Make one animal at a time, show it, then continue.
